Drainage & Grading for Carthage yards

Carthage soil is the classic East Texas setup, sandy loam on top and red clay underneath, so rain soaks in fast, hits the clay and moves sideways toward the slab and the low corner of the yard. We fix it with regrading, French drains in sock-wrapped pipe and clean stone, and downspout reroutes that run to a real outlet. Most projects run $2,000 to $3,750.

Half the yards we get called to for a French drain don't need one. Standing water is a symptom, so we start by finding the source. Roof water dumping at the foundation gets rerouted in solid pipe. Ground that tips back toward the house gets regraded to fall away, about six inches over the first ten feet. Only water that's trapped in the ground calls for a French drain.

How the job goes

We fix drainage year-round, but the best time to see the problem is during or right after a rain. Digging goes faster in a dry stretch, when the clay isn't saturated. Getting it done before the spring rains means you don't spend another season with a pond in the back corner.

  1. Watch the water

    We walk the property during or right after a rain when we can, and trace where the water comes from and where it sits.

  2. Pick the right fix

    Downspouts, regrading, a swale or a French drain. You get a written, itemized estimate that says what we're installing and why.

  3. Trench to a real outlet

    We dig with a steady fall toward somewhere the water can actually leave: daylight, a curb, a lower part of the lot, or a pop-up emitter.

  4. Build the drain

    Pipe, fabric and stone go in to spec, backfilled with stone so clay fines can't clog it.

  5. Put the yard back

    We cap the trench with soil and sod, or finish it in river rock or a dry creek bed.

How much does a French drain cost in East Texas?

Most of our drainage projects run $2,000 to $3,750. A standard single French drain run is usually $1,900 to $3,000. Small fixes like a downspout reroute run $450 to $1,000, full systems $3,000 to $4,500, and large or combined projects $4,500 and up. Pipe length, dig depth, the outlet and what goes back on top set the number.

Do I really need a French drain?

Maybe not. Most soggy yards are fed by surface water: roof runoff, a neighbor's lot that sits higher, or a driveway sloping back toward the house. Those get fixed with downspout reroutes and regrading, for less money. A French drain is for water already in the ground, like a spot that stays spongy for days, a seep at the bottom of a slope, or the back of a retaining wall.

Why does my Longview yard hold water after it rains?

Our soil. Most yards here are sandy loam over dense red clay that water can't soak through, so once the top foot saturates, the water sits or runs sideways. Settled grading, downspouts that dump at the foundation and dead-flat lots make it worse. We trace the flow path first, then fix the source instead of guessing.
