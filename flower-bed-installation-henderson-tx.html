Flower Bed Installation for Henderson yards

For shady Henderson beds under pines and oaks, azaleas and hydrangeas do well when they get morning sun and afternoon shade. Sunny beds get knockout roses, salvia, lantana and plumbago that hold up through August. Every new bed gets a clean edge, amended soil, a grade that moves water away from the house, and mulch at an inch to an inch and a half.

Most beds that die here didn't fail because of the plants. They failed because of what's under them. So every install starts with prep: a clean bed line that won't wander into the lawn, every weed pulled by the root, the top of the soil loosened, compost worked into heavy clay, and a grade that moves water off instead of letting it sit around the plants.

How the job goes

Shrubs and perennials do best planted in fall, September through November, while the soil is warm and the air is cooling. Heat lovers like vinca should wait until the soil is truly warm, usually mid-May and later, because a cool, wet spring rots them. Seasonal color rotates with the weather, so the bed has something blooming most of the year.

  1. Walk the bed

    We check how many hours of sun the bed gets, how it drains after a rain, and what you want to see from the street and the porch.

  2. Plan the plants

    You get a written estimate with plant counts, sizes and the edging spec. If you want to see it drawn first, our $199 Flower Bed Preview gives you a rendering.

  3. Prep the ground

    We cut the edge, pull weeds by the root, loosen the top of the soil and work in compost where the clay is heavy.

  4. Plant and mulch

    Plants go in with real spacing so air moves through them, then mulch goes down at 1 to 1.5 inches, pulled back off the stems.

  5. Hand it over

    We clean up and walk you through watering: deep and less often, so the roots go down instead of rotting in wet clay.

Flower Bed Installation questions in Henderson

Why do my flower beds in Henderson die every August?

Usually it's the soil, not the plants. Compacted ground, beds that hold water, or mulch piled too deep will finish off plants in our heat. We fix the prep first, then plant for the sun the bed actually gets.

How much does a new flower bed cost in Longview, TX?

Flower beds fall under our landscaping and planting work, where most projects run $900 to $3,600. A small refresh of an existing bed starts at a few hundred dollars. The size of the bed, how many plants and what size, the edging material and how much prep the soil needs set the price. We walk it for free and send a written, itemized quote.

What flowers survive the summer in East Texas?

In full sun, lantana, annual vinca, salvia, plumbago and knockout roses hold their color through July and August. All five want sun and good drainage and hate being overwatered. Lantana and plumbago are often root-hardy here and come back in spring. Vinca should wait for warm soil, usually mid-May on, or it can rot in a cool, wet spring.

Why did my new flower bed die in its first summer?

Usually it's the plant choice or the prep, not you. Spring annuals like petunias and impatiens aren't built for our heat. Beds set on compacted clay with weeds left underneath struggle from day one, and daily watering in clay drowns roots. Most beds we redo for clients failed on the prep step. The plants were fine. The soil they sat in wasn't.
