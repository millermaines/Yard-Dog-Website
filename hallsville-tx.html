Hallsville yards cover a wide range. In town and in the newer subdivisions you get standard lots with builder beds and a line of pines or hardwoods along the back. What Hallsville yards deal with

Subdivision lots next to acreage

Hallsville has newer neighborhood lots and country acreage that backs up to pasture or woods. Mowing, edging and leaf work get priced to the actual ground, not a flat town rate.

Flat backyards that hold water

Newer homes sit on graded pads, and once the topsoil settles, a flat backyard can hold water for a day after a hard rain. That usually calls for regrading, a swale and a pop-up emitter before anyone needs a French drain.

Sandy high ground, clay low spots

Higher ground tends to be sandy loam that drains quickly and dries out in summer. Low spots sit on heavier clay, which changes how we prep the ground for sod and new beds.

Tree lines on the back of the lot

Many Hallsville lots have pines and hardwoods along the back or side lines. They shade the grass closest to them and drop needles and leaves into the lawn from fall into winter.

How much does weekly mowing cost in Hallsville?

On a typical residential lot, weekly mowing runs about $50 to $65 per visit. Acreage, heavy tree lines and a lot of trimming along fences move that number, so bigger Hallsville properties get priced after we walk them. Either way you get a flat per-visit price in writing before we start.

My backyard stays soggy after it rains. Is that something you fix?

Yes, and it's common on newer Hallsville lots. Standing water usually comes from roof runoff, a grade that slopes the wrong way, or water trapped on the clay layer, and we find out which before we price anything. Small fixes like rerouting a downspout run $450 to $1,000, and most drainage projects run $2,000 to $3,750.
