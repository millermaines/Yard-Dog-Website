Henderson is the Rusk County seat and sits in the old East Texas Oil Field country, and a lot of its neighborhoods have been settling in for decades. Older lots tend to be generous, with established oaks, pines and dogwoods, and azalea and camellia beds that need real pruning rather than a quick shear. What Henderson yards deal with

Established trees on older lots

Older Henderson neighborhoods have big oaks, pines and dogwoods that shade the lawn and drop a lot of litter. The grass under them is usually St. Augustine, and it thins wherever the light runs short.

Azaleas and camellias that need a plan

Mature azaleas and camellias show up in a lot of Henderson beds. They get pruned right after bloom, because a shear in fall or winter costs you next spring's flowers.

Acreage outside town

Past the edge of town, Henderson properties open into acreage with pasture and pine woods. We price those on the walkthrough, since mowing and leaf work scale with the ground, not the house.

Low spots that stay wet

Sandy topsoil over clay drains fast until the clay stops it. Low corners, flat backyards and beds against the slab are where Henderson yards hold water after a storm.
