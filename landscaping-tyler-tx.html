How the job goes

Fall is the best planting window in East Texas. From September to about Thanksgiving the soil stays warm while the air cools, so trees and shrubs build roots for months before they see summer heat. Spring works for summer color once the soil warms up, but anything woody planted in April spends its first summer just trying to survive.

  1. Walk the property

    We look at sun, shade, slope and where water goes after a rain, and talk through what you want the yard to do.

  2. Write the plan and price

    You get a written, itemized estimate with plants, sizes and materials, usually within a day. If you want to see it drawn first, a Design Preview gives you renderings before anything goes in the ground.

  3. Prep the ground

    We cut the bed lines, pull weeds by the root, loosen compacted soil and work compost into heavy clay where it needs it.

  4. Plant and finish

    Trees and shrubs go in with the root flare at grade, never buried. Then the beds get mulch or rock and a clean edge.

  5. Clean up and walk it with you

    We haul off the debris, blow off the walks and drive, and go over how to water the new plants through their first season.

When is the best time to plant shrubs and trees in East Texas?

Fall. From September through November the soil is still warm while the air cools off, and roots keep growing through most of our mild winter. A shrub planted in early October gets months of root growth before summer. One planted in April gets about eight weeks before the first 100-degree day. Keep watering through winter, especially before a hard freeze.
