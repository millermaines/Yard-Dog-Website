How the job goes

Mowing season in East Texas runs from about late March through October, with weekly cuts during peak growth and every other week as it slows. In peak growing weather a lawn wants a cut every five to seven days so you only ever take the top third. Starting in spring keeps the lawn out of the scalp-and-recover cycle that wrecks it by August.

  1. Walk the yard

    We look at your grass type, the sun, the slopes and the obstacles, then send a written per-visit price, usually within a day.

  2. Set the schedule

  3. Mow, edge, trim, blow

  4. Adjust with the season

    We raise the deck for summer heat, mulch the first fall leaves back into the lawn, and drop to every other week when the grass slows down.

How often should my lawn be mowed in East Texas?

Weekly from about late March through October for most yards, when bermuda and St. Augustine put on the most growth, then every other week as things slow down. In peak growing weather a lawn wants a cut every five to seven days so you never take more than a third of the blade at once. We'll recommend a schedule after we walk your yard.

What height should I mow bermuda and St. Augustine?

Bermuda can run one to one and a half inches in spring and fall, but in a July heat wave hold it around two to two and a half inches. St. Augustine should never go below three inches and wants three and a half to four through summer. Cutting St. Augustine like bermuda is one of the surest ways to open a bare patch.
