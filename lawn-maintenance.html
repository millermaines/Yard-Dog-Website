What we do

Lawn maintenance is the basics done the same way every week. We mow, edge along the walks, driveway and beds, string-trim around everything the mower can't reach, and blow every hard surface clean. Clippings go back into the turf instead of into bags, where they hand a little nitrogen and moisture back to the soil.

The part most crews skip is the cut itself. We set the deck for the grass you have, not one height for every yard. Bermuda runs lower, St. Augustine never drops under three inches, and both go up in July. We take no more than a third of the blade at a time, keep blades sharp so the grass is sliced instead of torn, and change mowing direction so the turf doesn't rut. Same crew every visit, so the people mowing your yard in August are the ones who noticed the thin spot in May.

How the job goes

Mowing season in East Texas runs from about late March through October, with weekly cuts during peak growth and every other week as it slows. In peak growing weather a lawn wants a cut every five to seven days so you only ever take the top third. Starting in spring keeps the lawn out of the scalp-and-recover cycle that wrecks it by August.

  1. Walk the yard

    We look at your grass type, the sun, the slopes and the obstacles, then send a written per-visit price, usually within a day.

  2. Set the schedule

    Most yards go weekly from late March through October and every other week as growth slows. The same crew works your yard every time.

  3. Mow, edge, trim, blow

    Every visit gets the full routine: deck set for your grass, clean edges, trimmed obstacles and hard surfaces blown off.

  4. Adjust with the season

    We raise the deck for summer heat, mulch the first fall leaves back into the lawn, and drop to every other week when the grass slows down.

  5. Flag what we see

    Thin spots, standing water or a patch that keeps browning. We tell you while it is still small and cheap to fix.

July is when mowing height decides everything

Short grass grows short roots. Scalp a lawn to save a mow in July and the roots pull back to match, right when the top inch of our clay bakes dry by early afternoon. The shortest-mowed yards on the street are always the first ones to go crispy.

So we mow by grass type. Bermuda can run one to one and a half inches in spring, then goes up to about two to two and a half for summer. St. Augustine stays at three and a half to four inches all summer and never drops under three. If your yard has both, we cut them at different heights instead of splitting the difference. A taller stand also shades the soil, which keeps it cooler and holds water longer.

We walk the yard and give you a flat per-visit price in writing before the first mow, so there are no surprises on the bill.

How often should my lawn be mowed in East Texas?

Weekly from about late March through October for most yards, when bermuda and St. Augustine put on the most growth, then every other week as things slow down. In peak growing weather a lawn wants a cut every five to seven days so you never take more than a third of the blade at once. We'll recommend a schedule after we walk your yard.

What height should I mow bermuda and St. Augustine?

Bermuda can run one to one and a half inches in spring and fall, but in a July heat wave hold it around two to two and a half inches. St. Augustine should never go below three inches and wants three and a half to four through summer. Cutting St. Augustine like bermuda is one of the surest ways to open a bare patch.

Do you bag the grass clippings?

No. We blow clippings back into the turf instead of bagging or hauling them off. Dry clippings break down fast and hand a little nitrogen and moisture back to the soil. Walks, drives and patios get blown clean every visit, so nothing ends up tracked into the house or washed into the street.

Will it be the same crew every time?

Yes. Yard Dog is Miller Maines and a crew of four, and the same people work your yard every visit. We never subcontract. That matters for mowing more than people expect, because a crew that knows your yard knows where the soft spots are, which beds need a careful edge and how high your grass should be cut.
