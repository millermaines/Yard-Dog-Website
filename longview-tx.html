Who does landscaping and lawn care in Longview, TX?

Yard Dog Landscapes is a family-owned landscaping and lawn care company based in Longview, TX. Miller Maines started it in 2017, and Miller and the same crew of four still do every job. We never subcontract. We're fully insured and hold a 5.0 rating on Google from 143 reviews. Every project starts with a free walkthrough and a written, itemized estimate, usually within a day, and you can request one anytime.

Longview is the Gregg County seat, and the east side of town crosses into Harrison County. Most yards here are sandy loam with red clay underneath. The sand dries out fast in August and the clay holds water after a spring storm, so the same lot can be thirsty in summer and soggy in April. Older streets around downtown sit under tall pines, post oaks and pecans that shade the grass and fill the beds every fall. Newer lots on the edges of town start with thin topsoil over a graded pad, young trees and full sun, and they shed a lot of water when it rains.

Longview is home base, so the shop is never far from your yard. We work Spring Hill, Pine Tree, Greggton, the neighborhoods around downtown and everywhere in between. Being close keeps estimates quick and makes it easy to stop back after a heavy rain to check a new drain or a fresh bed. We don't promise a fixed route day, but we'll tell you up front how often we'll be out and hold to it.

What Longview yards deal with

Sand on top, red clay underneath

Water runs through Longview's sandy topsoil fast, then stalls on the clay below. That's why low spots stay soggy for days after a storm while beds dry out after a single week of August heat.

Shade from old pines and oaks

Established neighborhoods carry tall pines, post oaks and pecans. St. Augustine holds up in that shade far better than Bermuda, and beds under the canopy need shade plants and more mulch.

Rolling lots move water

Longview isn't flat. Runoff from the street or the lot next door often ends up against a foundation or in a back corner, which is where regrading, French drains and short walls pay for themselves.

Leaves and needles most of the year

Pines drop needles off and on all year, then oaks, pecans and sweetgums let go from November into December. Beds and lawns under them fill up faster than most people plan for.
