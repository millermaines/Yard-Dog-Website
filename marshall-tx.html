Marshall is the Harrison County seat, and a lot of its homes have been standing a long time. Near downtown and in the older neighborhoods you find mature magnolias, pecans and oaks, azaleas that have been in the ground for decades, and beds that have been replanted over and over. Those yards need patient work: shaping old shrubs instead of shearing them, edging around big roots, and pruning at the right time of year. Newer homes and country lots on the edges of town are a different job, with more open turf and more sun.

The ground here is the same sandy topsoil over red clay as the rest of our area, so water that can't soak in sits on top. We look at drainage and shade on every walkthrough, then quote the work in writing. Marshall is part of our regular service area along with Hallsville, so getting out to see your yard is easy. We set the schedule after we've seen the property, rather than promising a day of the week up front.

What Marshall yards deal with

Old plantings near downtown

Older Marshall homes often have azaleas, camellias, boxwoods and magnolias that went in the ground decades ago. They get pruned by hand at the right time, not sheared flat in one pass.

Heavy shade, thin grass

Big oaks and pecans shade a lot of older Marshall lawns, and bermuda gives up under that kind of canopy. Under the deepest trees, St. Augustine, a shade bed or plain mulch is usually the honest answer.

Sandy top, red clay below

Rain soaks into the sandy topsoil fast, then stops at the clay and spreads sideways. That's why low corners and flat beds near the foundation stay wet after a storm.

More room outside town

Toward Scottsville, Karnack and the county roads, lots open up into acreage with pasture and woods. Those get priced on the walkthrough because the time on site is so different.
