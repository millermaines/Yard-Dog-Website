Nacogdoches is deeper in the Piney Woods than Longview, and the yards show it. The town is hillier, with streets that roll down toward La Nana and Banita creeks, and plenty of lots have a real grade to them. Tall pines and hardwoods shade the older neighborhoods around downtown and the Stephen F. Austin campus, and the soil runs acidic. This is the town of the Azalea Trail and the big azalea garden at SFA, and azaleas, dogwoods and camellias do well here for good reason. St. Augustine carries the lawns in the shade, and Bermuda only holds on the open, sunny lots.

What Nacogdoches yards deal with

Real slopes

Nacogdoches is hillier than most of our service area. Sloped yards shed water fast and wash out beds, which is where walls, terraces and drainage earn their keep.

Acidic soil, azalea country

Pine needles and rain keep the soil on the acidic side. Azaleas, camellias and dogwoods love it, while lawns often need a soil test and lime more than extra fertilizer.

Heavy shade on older streets

Tall pines and hardwoods shade much of the older town near downtown and campus. St. Augustine is the grass that holds up there, and the deepest shade is better as a bed than a lawn.

Azaleas set the pruning calendar

Azaleas set next year's flower buds soon after they bloom in spring. Any shaping has to happen right after bloom, or you cut off next year's show.
