Yard Dog's site isn't badly built — it's uniformly built. Nine sections, one shape: centered heading, two-fragment headline with a red word at the end, grid of equal rounded cards. That sameness is what reads as machine-made. Meanwhile the strongest asset on the property — documented, named, before-and-after job sequences — is cropped into 112-pixel thumbnails. This roadmap keeps every good bone, fixes the rhythm, and puts the evidence in front.

01Diagnosis

Seven tells, and none of them are
"the design is ugly."

"AI slop" isn't a look — it's a set of habits. Every one below is a pattern a language model reaches for when it has no strong opinion, and every one is fixable without touching the infrastructure underneath.

01

Headline monoculture Loudest tell

All seven homepage H2s are the same rhetorical device — two clipped fragments joined by a period — and each one paints its final word red. Read in sequence, it stops sounding like a company and starts sounding like a generator with one trick.

Evidence · index.html

"The full pack of outdoor services." · "Loyal as a hound. Sharp as a spade." · "A look at the backyard." · "Four steps. Zero guesswork." · "What our neighbors say." · "Straight answers. No runaround." · "Ready to throw us the bone?"

02

One section shape, nine times Structural

Every section is py-24 lg:py-32, centered heading, centered subhead, then an even grid of rounded cards on alternating cream / white / navy. Identical spacing means identical emphasis: the FAQ gets exactly as much room and weight as the finished-work gallery.

Hierarchy is what a designer chooses. When nothing is bigger, the reader concludes nobody chose.

03

The best asset is the smallest element Highest cost

There are four complete job sequences on disk — Logan King's checkerboard paver patio, Kerrie Medlin's flagstone patio, Robert Jackson's driveway retaining wall, Janessa Glenn's beds, deck and fence — each shot before, during base prep, mid-install, and finished. Almost no competing landscaper in East Texas has that. On the site they appear as small landscape-cropped tiles in a card grid.

Worse, they're portrait — 960×1280 and 1200×1600 phone photos — being force-cropped into wide cards, so the top and bottom of every transformation is thrown away.

04

Twenty testimonials, none legible Trust leak

The reviews section renders twenty identical t-card tiles at roughly 11px. Volume is doing the arguing, which backfires — a wall of tiny uniform quotes is what scraped testimonials look like. Three real reviews at readable size, each attached to the job photo it describes, will outperform twenty.

05

The dog metaphor at maximum density Voice

"Sit. Stay. Perfect Landscape." is the sanctioned tagline and it's genuinely good — keep it. But the site also runs "the full pack," "loyal as a hound," "sharp as a spade," "throw us the bone," and a bone icon. A joke told once is brand. Told six times on one page, it's filler where an argument should be.

06

The photo watermarks are still the old green brand Found while building this

The logo burned into the job photos is rendered in the retired April 2026 green — sampled at roughly #29692D, effectively the old Yard Dog Green #2E5E2E. It sits in the bottom corner of 29 photographs.

That includes every single frame of all three named job sequences — Janessa Glenn's beds, deck and fence; Kerrie Medlin's flagstone patio; Robert Jackson's driveway wall. Precisely the photographs the new Our Work section is built on. A red-branded site whose strongest evidence carries a green logo undercuts itself in the one place it can least afford to.

_scripts/rewatermark-bwafter.mjs already exists, so the tooling pattern is there — this is a re-render, not a rebuild. Cheap, and it belongs in Phase 0 alongside the token work.

07

Decorative chrome standing in for substance Craft

Floating checkmark pills and a "Booking February" badge hover over the hero photo; card backgrounds carry pink gradient washes that mean nothing; a marquee scrolls unfalsifiable phrases. Each is ornament where information belongs. The hero has room for real numbers — 5.0 stars, 100+ Google reviews, since 2017, licensed and insured — and they'd do more work.

02Do not break

What's genuinely good here.

This is the part most redesigns destroy. The visual layer is replaceable; the machinery under it represents real, compounding investment and stays exactly where it is.

The local SEO surface

11 services × 11 cities = 121 generated pages, plus hubs, sitemap, feeds, and JSON-LD. Every URL stays. New templates get regenerated through the existing _scripts/gen-niche-*.mjs so all 121 inherit at once.

The AEO / GEO pipeline

npm run seo:upgrade unifies the LocalBusiness graph and regenerates llms.txt, llms-full.txt and robots.txt. It stays the canonical last step of every build.

The Jobber booking pipeline

The quote form is a real embedded Jobber work request, not a mailto. Requests land in the same system that produces quotes, appointments and invoices. Keep the embed — expose more of what it can already do.

Anton + Inter

Correct per the brand guide, and a genuinely strong pairing — condensed display against a neutral grotesque. Nothing generic about it. The problem is scale discipline, not the fonts.

The blog, and its cadence

Thirteen posts on chinch bugs, fall armyworms, brown patch vs heat stress, crape murder. That's real regional expertise on a weekly autopublish schedule. It feeds both search and the case for competence.

Navy + cream + red

Brand Black #0F1B2A against Cream #F4ECD8 with red is a confident, uncommon ground for this category — most competitors are green-on-white. The palette is right; its application is what needs work.

03Design system

Red is right. Gold is
the missing half.

There are two brand guides on disk. The green one is v1.0, April 2026. The red one is v1.0, May 2026 — newer, and corroborated by the physical brand: the crew wears red YD shirts and hoodies, the mower is red, the F-350 is branded. So the red is deliberate and current, and it stays.

The real color finding

The May guide ships eight colors. The site uses three of them, invents four that aren't in any guide (sage #E5DCB3, khaki #D9C79A, mocha #5A3A22, red #C8362C), and never once uses Accent Gold #D4A437.

That absence is the direct cause of tell #01. With red as the only accent, every heading has to reach for red to create emphasis — so red stops meaning anything. Gold gives the system a second voice: ratings, star counts, numerals, "booking now" tags, years-in-business. Red gets demoted to what it's for — the primary action, and only that.

Separately, and strictly as maintenance: the tokens are misnamed. styles.css declares --color-green-primary: #E63946 and the Tailwind config declares green: '#E63946', // Yard Dog Green. The values are correct; the names lie about them. Rename to --yd-red / ydg.red during Phase 0 so the next person doesn't "fix" the color back to green.

Rhythm, depth, motion

Three spacing tiers, not one. Anchor sections (hero, Our Work) get 140px blocks; supporting sections (services, process, reviews) get 96px; utility sections (FAQ, area, footer) get 64px. The reader should feel where the important parts are before reading a word.

Depth by role, not decoration. Three planes only — cream ground, white elevated, navy anchored. Shadows tint with Mascot Brown rather than neutral black, so elevation reads warm instead of grey. No gradient washes inside cards; a gradient must either be a photo scrim or a light source, never a texture.

Motion earns its place. Transform and opacity only, 180–260ms, spring easing on entrances and smooth on exits. Before/after reveals and the sticky call bar are the only animated behaviours; everything else is static. Full prefers-reduced-motion honouring.

05Voice

Five rules that do most
of the de-slopping.

The layout changes are the visible half. This is the half that actually removes the machine-written feel — and it's enforceable, so it survives the next round of generated pages.

01
One dog metaphor per page. It's the tagline.

"Sit. Stay. Perfect Landscape." earns its place in the hero. After that, write about grass.

Now"The full pack of outdoor services."
Next"What we do"
02
No heading may be two fragments joined by a period.

This single rule breaks the strongest tell on the site. A heading is a label or a sentence — pick one.

Now"Four steps. Zero guesswork."
Next"How a job actually goes"
03
No accent-coloured last word.

Red is for the primary button. Gold carries numerals and proof. Neither colour highlights a word inside a heading, ever.

04
Every claim carries a number, a name, or gets cut.

"Transparent pricing" is a claim anyone can type. "From $18/sq ft installed" is one only someone who does the work can.

Now"Reliable crews who show up on time, every time."
Next"Same three-person crew on your property since 2017."
05
Answer the question in the first sentence.

Applies to FAQ answers, service intros, and blog openings. It's also precisely what AEO and GEO extraction rewards, so the voice fix and the search fix are the same fix.

Two things I'd want your call on before Phase 1

1 · Pricing. The services lineup is much stronger with honest "from" prices, and it's the question every visitor arrives with. The figures in the mockup are placeholders. If you'd rather not publish numbers, the pattern still works with "Free estimate" in that slot — but it gives up most of the advantage.

2 · Named customers. The Our Work section is far more persuasive with real first names and cities, which is how the photos are already filed. Worth confirming those customers are comfortable being named on the site before it goes live.
