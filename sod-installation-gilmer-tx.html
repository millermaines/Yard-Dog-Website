Sod Installation for Gilmer yards

When a Gilmer lawn has been scalped, burned out or overrun, sod is the clean restart. Sand lets roots move quickly, but it also lets water drain away, so we strip the old turf, work compost in to hold moisture, level the bed and lay Bermuda in sun or St. Augustine in shade. Sod is priced by the pallet, and most projects run $1,500 to $4,700.

Sod takes or fails because of what happens before the pallets show up. We strip whatever is there down to bare dirt, dead turf and weeds included, regrade where water needs to move, and till in two to three inches of compost or topsoil. Then we level and roll the bed so the sod sits flat.

How the job goes

Bermuda goes down best from late April through August, and St. Augustine from May through September, when the ground is warm. St. Augustine needs four to six weeks of root growth before dormancy, so get it in by the end of September. Laid in November, it sits on the ground like a rug.

  1. Map the sun

    We walk the yard, note where the sun actually falls in summer, and recommend a grass, or a split, for each area.

  2. Measure and quote

    We measure the square footage and send a written quote priced by the pallet.

  3. Prep the ground

    Old turf and weeds come out, the grade gets set, and compost or topsoil is tilled in, leveled and rolled.

  4. Lay it tight

    Sod goes down with staggered seams and no gaps, over starter fertilizer.

  5. Water it in

    You get a daily morning watering schedule for the first two weeks, then a taper to deep, less frequent soaks once the roots take hold.

Sod Installation questions in Gilmer

Does sod take in sandy soil around Gilmer?

It takes well if it gets water. Roots move through sand quickly, but the sod dries out just as fast, so it needs deep watering every morning for the first two weeks. We work compost in before laying so the soil holds moisture longer.

Which sod is best for East Texas?

It depends on sun. Bermuda needs six to eight hours of direct sun and handles heat, kids and dogs better than anything. St. Augustine is the shade grass and holds on with four to six hours. Zoysia sits in between. Under four hours, no turf lasts. Plenty of yards should be split, and we map the sun before we recommend one.

How often should I water new sod?

Every morning for the first two weeks or so, deep enough to soak through the sod into the soil below, until the roots grab. After that, taper to two deep waterings a week that finish around sunrise, about an inch a week in summer. Evening watering keeps the blades wet all night, which invites fungus. We leave you a written schedule.
