How the job goes

Bermuda goes down best from late April through August, and St. Augustine from May through September, when the ground is warm. St. Augustine needs four to six weeks of root growth before dormancy, so get it in by the end of September. Laid in November, it sits on the ground like a rug.

  1. Map the sun

    We walk the yard, note where the sun actually falls in summer, and recommend a grass, or a split, for each area.

  2. Measure and quote

    We measure the square footage and send a written quote priced by the pallet.

  3. Prep the ground

    Old turf and weeds come out, the grade gets set, and compost or topsoil is tilled in, leveled and rolled.

  4. Lay it tight

    Sod goes down with staggered seams and no gaps, over starter fertilizer.

  5. Water it in

    You get a daily morning watering schedule for the first two weeks, then a taper to deep, less frequent soaks once the roots take hold.

Sod Installation questions in Hallsville

Should I put down bermuda or St. Augustine in my Hallsville yard?

It comes down to sun. Bermuda needs six or more hours of direct sun and handles kids and dogs well. St. Augustine gets by on four to six hours of dappled light, which is what you get near a tree line. Plenty of yards end up with bermuda out front and St. Augustine in the shaded back.

Which sod is best for East Texas?

It depends on sun. Bermuda needs six to eight hours of direct sun and handles heat, kids and dogs better than anything. St. Augustine is the shade grass and holds on with four to six hours. Zoysia sits in between. Under four hours, no turf lasts. Plenty of yards should be split, and we map the sun before we recommend one.

How often should I water new sod?

Every morning for the first two weeks or so, deep enough to soak through the sod into the soil below, until the roots grab. After that, taper to two deep waterings a week that finish around sunrise, about an inch a week in summer. Evening watering keeps the blades wet all night, which invites fungus.
