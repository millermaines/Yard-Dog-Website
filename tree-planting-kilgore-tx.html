Tree Planting for Kilgore yards

Kilgore's sandy ground is easy to dig and drains freely, so new trees here mostly need steady water their first couple of summers. We plant in fall when we can, set the root ball at grade or a touch high and finish with a basin and mulch ring. Tree planting is priced once we've walked the site with you.

Planting too deep is the most common way we see a new tree killed in East Texas, and it takes two or three years to finish the job, so nobody traces it back to planting day. We set the root flare, where the trunk widens into its first roots, at grade or an inch above. On container trees that flare is often buried a few inches under the potting soil, so we measure to the flare, not the top of the pot.

How the job goes

Fall is the best window in East Texas, from September until around Thanksgiving. The soil stays warm for weeks after the air cools, so a tree planted in October gets close to six months of root growth before summer, while one planted in April gets about eight weeks. We plant year-round, but a spring tree needs more watering through its first summer.

  1. Read the spot

    We look at sun, drainage, nearby structures and how big the tree will get, then recommend species that fit.

  2. Source the tree

    We pick healthy stock from regional growers, and we'd usually rather plant a smaller tree that roots in fast.

  3. Dig and set

    Wide, shallow hole, root flare at grade, circling roots cut and native soil back in.

  4. Basin, mulch and stake

    We build a watering basin, lay a mulch ring off the trunk and stake only if the wind calls for it.

  5. Water and check in

    You get a written watering schedule, and on bigger installs we come back around the 30-day mark to check establishment.

Tree Planting questions in Kilgore

Can I plant a tree under the big pecans in my Kilgore yard?

Small understory trees like redbud or dogwood can work at the edge of a canopy. Planting tight under a mature pecan or oak means fighting its roots for water and light, and digging there can damage those roots. We'll find a spot where the new tree can grow without hurting the old one.

When is the best time to plant a tree in East Texas?

Fall, from September until around Thanksgiving. The soil stays warm long after the air cools, so roots keep growing through most of our winter, and the tree has months of root under it before the first 100-degree day. Spring planting works but needs more watering all summer. Bare-root fruit trees and roses go in while dormant, December through February.

What trees grow best in East Texas?

Live oak, Shumard oak, post oak, southern magnolia, bald cypress, red maple, river birch, Chinese pistache, redbud, hollies and crepe myrtles all do well here. What struggles are maples bred for the Midwest and fruit trees from cooler climates. Mature size matters as much as species. A Natchez crepe myrtle wants 25 to 30 feet, while an Acoma stays near 10.
