How the job goes

Late winter is the main pruning window here. Crepe myrtles get thinned in February while they're dormant and before the buds break, and knockout roses get cut back hard the same month. Spring bloomers like azaleas and camellias wait until right after they flower, and oaks are left alone through spring and early summer because of oak wilt.

  1. Walk the property

    We identify each tree and shrub, flag what needs work and what should be left alone, and send a written quote.

  2. Schedule by species

    Crepe myrtles and knockout roses go in February, azaleas right after bloom, hollies and boxwoods in early summer, and oaks outside spring and early summer.

  3. Make clean cuts

    Cuts go at the branch collar, sized to what the plant can close. We thin instead of topping.

  4. Clean up and haul off

    Limbs, trimmings and sawdust are raked up and hauled away before we leave.

  5. Follow through

    Recovery work like a topped crepe myrtle takes two or three winters, so we plan it out and come back each dormant season to finish it.

When should crepe myrtles be pruned in East Texas?

February, while the tree is dormant and before the buds break. Take out suckers, crossing branches and dead twigs, and keep cuts up top to pencil or finger thickness. Don't top the trunks back to knuckles. A crepe myrtle blooms on new wood on its own. In summer, just snip spent flower clusters to push a second round of bloom.

Can a topped crepe myrtle be fixed?

Yes. The gentle way is to go knuckle by knuckle each February, keep the two or three strongest shoots and cut the rest flush. In three or four years the knuckles disappear into the canopy. If the knuckles have rot in them, the faster fix is cutting the tree to the ground and keeping three to five new shoots as trunks. You lose a year of bloom.
