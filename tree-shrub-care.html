What we do

Tree and shrub care starts with knowing what you're looking at. Live oaks, post oaks, crepe myrtles, hollies, azaleas and boxwoods all want different timing and different cuts. We prune for the plant's health first, then for the look you want, and we make our cuts at the branch collar so the wound can close.

We're not the crew that tops crepe myrtles into stumps. We thin them, take out crossing and rubbing branches, and limb them up so the bark shows, and we bring back trees that were topped for years. We also clear dead lower branches that shade the lawn, take out small trees and shrubs that have died or outgrown their spot, refresh the mulch around tree bases, and haul off every limb when we leave.

How the job goes

Late winter is the main pruning window here. Crepe myrtles get thinned in February while they're dormant and before the buds break, and knockout roses get cut back hard the same month. Spring bloomers like azaleas and camellias wait until right after they flower, and oaks are left alone through spring and early summer because of oak wilt.

  1. Walk the property

    We identify each tree and shrub, flag what needs work and what should be left alone, and send a written quote.

  2. Schedule by species

    Crepe myrtles and knockout roses go in February, azaleas right after bloom, hollies and boxwoods in early summer, and oaks outside spring and early summer.

  3. Make clean cuts

    Cuts go at the branch collar, sized to what the plant can close. We thin instead of topping.

  4. Clean up and haul off

    Limbs, trimmings and sawdust are raked up and hauled away before we leave.

  5. Follow through

    Recovery work like a topped crepe myrtle takes two or three winters, so we plan it out and come back each dormant season to finish it.

Oak wilt and crape murder are local problems

East Texas is oak country, and we have oak wilt. The fungus spreads partly through beetles drawn to the fresh sap of a pruning cut, and they're most active in spring and early summer. We prune oaks in the dead of winter or the heat of late summer. When a storm-broken oak limb has to come off, we cut it clean and paint the wound with pruning sealer right away, the one case where sealing a cut helps.

Drive down Judson Road in August and you can pick out the topped crepe myrtles by their tight mop of flowers at one height. Topping makes the tree grow back taller on weaker wood. A Natchez wants 25 to 30 feet no matter how it's cut. If the tree is wrong for the spot, the fix is a smaller variety, not a saw.

When should crepe myrtles be pruned in East Texas?

February, while the tree is dormant and before the buds break. Take out suckers, crossing branches and dead twigs, and keep cuts up top to pencil or finger thickness. Don't top the trunks back to knuckles. A crepe myrtle blooms on new wood on its own. In summer, just snip spent flower clusters to push a second round of bloom.

Can a topped crepe myrtle be fixed?

Yes. The gentle way is to go knuckle by knuckle each February, keep the two or three strongest shoots and cut the rest flush. In three or four years the knuckles disappear into the canopy. If the knuckles have rot in them, the faster fix is cutting the tree to the ground and keeping three to five new shoots as trunks. You lose a year of bloom.

What are the white webs in my pecan tree?

In late summer those are almost always fall webworms. On a mature shade pecan they're mostly cosmetic, since the tree is close to dropping its leaves anyway and comes back full in spring. If a pole pruner reaches the nest and the cut is one worth making, we prune it out. Young or stressed trees deserve a closer look. Never burn nests out of a tree.

Do you remove trees and stumps?

We remove small trees and shrubs that have died or outgrown their spot, and we clear storm debris and downed limbs. We don't take down large trees near the house or grind stumps. That is a job for a tree service with the right equipment, and we'll tell you so on the walkthrough instead of guessing at it.
